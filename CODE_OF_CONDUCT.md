# Code of Conduct

## Goal

FluxCast should be a project where anyone can report a bug, send a patch or ask
for help without being treated badly for it. That includes people who are new to
Linux, people whose English is shaky, and people who got something wrong.

## Standards

Three rules:

- Do not harass, attack or discriminate against anyone, including for their sex,
  religion, race, appearance, gender, identity, nationality, sexuality, age or
  disability.
- Stay on topic. No spam, no advertising, no trolling.
- Treat people with respect, especially when they do not understand something
  yet.

Breaking them looks like this:

- Insults, slurs, sexual attention, bullying, or publishing someone's private
  details.
- Posting unrelated content, promoting your own project in someone else's issue,
  arguing in bad faith to waste time.
- Talking down to someone for a basic question, or for a bug report that turned
  out to be their own misconfiguration.

Disagreeing is fine. Saying a patch is wrong is fine. Saying it bluntly is fine.
Saying the person who wrote it is an idiot is not.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project lives: issues, pull requests, code review and discussions
on GitHub, and the FluxCast Discord server. It also applies when you represent
the project somewhere else.

## Reporting

If something happened and nobody dealt with it, write to
[illia@fluxcast.dev](mailto:illia@fluxcast.dev).

I will not share your report or your name with anyone, including the person you
are reporting. Telling me is not the same as making it public.

## What happens then

I handle these case by case. Depending on what happened, the response is one of:

- A private word about what was wrong.
- A removed comment, a locked thread, or a closed issue.
- A temporary block from the repository or the Discord server.
- A permanent ban.

The decision is mine. If you think I got one wrong, say so in the same mail.
